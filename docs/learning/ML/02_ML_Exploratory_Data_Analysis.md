## A. Purpose and scope of the EDA

The EDA had four goals:
- assess the quality and lineage of `ML/Dataset/Processed/`;
- characterise the 3-class target `delay_status`;
- find pre-departure signal in each table;
- decide which features are safe, useful or leaking.

No models were trained in the EDA.

## B. Target analysis (notebook 02, sections B1–B5)

**Label integrity.** `delay_status` is an exact function of `actual_delay_hours` (0 disagreements). `actual_delay_hours` equals `actual_arrival_ts − expected_arrival_ts` exactly.

| Class | n | % | % (sea only) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| On-Time | 5,422 | 55.0 | 52.6 |
| At-Risk | 2,951 | 30.0 | 31.6 |
| Critical | 1,477 | 15.0 | 15.8 |

- **Distribution.** Delay is right-skewed: median 8.2 h, mean 18.3 h, p99 116 h, max 201 h. There is a sharp peak just below 0 h.
- **Early arrivals.** 39 % of all shipments arrive early (36 % of sea, 98 % of air), typically 2–7 h early. This follows from the generator's `delay * 0.3` for negative values ([01 · §B.5](01_ML_Dataset_Engineering.md#b5-shipments-and-the-delay-formula-section-8-lines-295366)).
- **Air is degenerate.** All 503 air shipments are On-Time; the maximum air delay is 3.4 h.
- **Threshold proximity.** 5.2 % of shipments lie within ±2 h of 12 h, and 2.1 % within ±2 h of 48 h. Labels there are sensitive to small timing errors. The evaluation later confirmed that errors cluster at these boundaries ([04 · §D](04_ML_Model_Evaluation.md#d-model-comparison)).
- **Temporal drift.** The weekly Critical share ranges from 8 % (week of 3 Feb) to 25 % (week of 20 Jan). The daily delayed share ranges from 26 % to 76 %.
- **Filled outcomes.** The 295 filled outcomes have the same distribution as observed ones (KS p = 0.58). That does not make them observations, so they are excluded ([01 · §C](01_ML_Dataset_Engineering.md#c-data-integration)).
- **Imbalance (B5).** The class mix is 55 / 30 / 15 (majority:minority 3.7:1). About 1,480 Critical examples are enough without oversampling. The classes are ordinal, errors are asymmetric (Critical → On-Time is the costliest), and **At-Risk is the hardest class**.

| Attribute | Evidence (sea) | Assessment |
|---|---|---|

## D. Port operations analysis (notebook 04, sections D1–D5)

- **Origin anchorage congestion is the strongest port-operations signal.** Median 24 h anchor mean is 0.42 (On-Time) → 0.67 (At-Risk) → 1.92 (Critical); δ Critical vs On-Time = 0.54 (large). The within-origin Spearman ρ with delay is positive at all 10 origins (0.09 DEHAM to 0.49 NLRTM).
- **Container dwell and vessel time in port** come next. Dwell median 78 → 88 → 101 h (δ 0.44); vessel time in port has δ 0.33. Both rise during disruptions (dwell δ inside vs outside events = 0.40).
- **Utilisation is non-monotonic in risk.** It equals realised throughput / nominal capacity, capped at 100 %, and it *falls* when capacity is lost: −24 pp during events and −38 pp during strikes. The current-hour value is therefore *lower* for Critical shipments (66 % vs 75 %), while the 24 h maximum is higher (96 % vs 90 %). Utilisation should be read together with queue length.
- `port_utilization_pct` = 100 in 1,628 port-hours (NLRTM in 41 % of its hours). This is saturated, censored throughput, not an error.
- **Negative results:**
  - Customs hold has no association with delay (ρ = 0.01 origin).
  - Equipment availability has no within-port link to throughput (ρ −0.05 to 0.13) and negligible signal (ε² = 0.005).
- **Labour status.** Strikes coincide with +2.9 vessels at anchor and +12 h dwell.
- **Destination conditions at departure** are weak in isolation (ε² ≤ 0.008). They nevertheless became a top permutation group in the models ([04 · §H](04_ML_Model_Evaluation.md#h-feature-importance-and-explainability)).
- **Hub utilisation** (`h_util_mean24`, ε² = 0.02) carries some signal on transshipment routes.

## E. Weather analysis (notebook 05, sections E1–E4)

- **Origin gusts in the prior 24 h** are the most informative weather variable. Median max gust is 34 / 36 / 49 km/h for On-Time / At-Risk / Critical (δ 0.44). The signal **survives within-port centring** (δ 0.45, ρ 0.26), so it is not a port proxy. Critical share rises sharply above about 50 km/h.
- **24 h precipitation** behaves almost identically (δ 0.44; within port 0.43).
- **Thunderstorm hours** have a pooled ρ of 0.34 that shrinks to 0.12 within port.
- **Weather mainly separates Critical.** At-Risk vs On-Time differences are small (δ ≈ 0.11–0.14).
- **Visibility** (δ −0.41) was filled during cleaning at USLAX, CNSHA and LKCMB. It is excluded from the model.
- Temperature, humidity and pressure levels mostly encode climate, that is, which port. The pressure anomaly keeps a small association (δ −0.29).
- **Wind direction** is a likely proxy for time or synoptic regime. It is treated as an overfitting risk.
- **Mechanism (observational).** Logged "high winds" events coincide with a median gust of 71 km/h (vs 24 km/h). Within port, gusts relate to lower utilisation (ρ −0.13) and longer queues (+0.10). This matches the generator's weather capacity factor ([01 · §B.3](01_ML_Dataset_Engineering.md#b3-port-hourly-telemetry-section-6-lines-228266)).
- Data-quality notes: wind gust < wind speed in 77 h, and gust < 0 in 2 h. Both are flagged and left unchanged.

## F. Network and route analysis (notebook 06, sections F1–F7)

- **Network.** It is a complete directed graph (90 routes), so connectivity itself does not vary. What varies is **route type** (52 direct, 38 transshipment) and the **hub**.
- **Transshipment.** 58.6 % delayed / 22.3 % Critical vs 40.7 % / 12.0 % on direct routes (V = 0.18). The gap holds in every distance quintile (+7 to +22 pp). All four hubs carry 55–61 % delayed; SGSIN-hubbed routes have the highest Critical share (25 %).
- **Distance and planned hours** are weak (δ 0.15–0.18).
- **Route identity is additive in its parts.** Origin + destination + hub explain **R² = 0.91** of route-level delay rates (adj. 0.88). The residual SD of 0.043 matches the binomial noise of 0.044. This is why `route_id` is excluded and routes are encoded through origin, destination and hub ([01 · §F](01_ML_Dataset_Engineering.md#f-leakage-prevention-and-prediction-cutoff)).
- **Port role matters.** NLRTM is the riskiest origin (68 %) but only average as a destination (54 %). INMAA is a low-risk origin (42 %) but the riskiest destination (59 %). Port effects are therefore encoded per role.
- **Routes.** 24 routes have CIs entirely above the sea average and 23 entirely below. The riskiest are LKCMB→INNSA (83 %), NLRTM→INNSA (83 %) and NLRTM→SGSIN (78 %).
- **Route frequency** shows no useful association (trailing 7-day departures ε² = 0.007).

## G. Disruptions and holidays (notebook 07, sections G1–G5)

**Disruptions.**
- There are 55 events: weather 27 (median 15 h), equipment failure 11, congestion 9 (median 61 h), strike 7 (median 56 h) and cyber 1.
- 7 events sit at exactly 85.0 % affected capacity, which matches the generator's `min(85, …)` cap.
- **An active origin disruption at departure is the single strongest signal.** Delayed / Critical shares by severity:

  | Severity | Delayed | Critical |
  |---|---:|---:|
  | none | 41 % | 10 % |
  | medium | 71 % | 24 % |
  | high | 96 % | 73 % |

  Severity has V = 0.33.
- By type: weather 98 % delayed / **86 % Critical** (n = 374), strike 85 / 40, congestion 77 / 33, equipment failure 48 / 11.
- Among the 1,431 shipments that depart during an origin event, affected capacity correlates with delay at ρ +0.60, and hours already elapsed at ρ −0.25. Both are knowable at departure.
- Remaining duration needs `event_end`, so it is **not** knowable at departure (ρ −0.12). It became a `LEAK_` column.
- **Events that start after departure** matter: 44 % vs 33 % delayed among shipments with no event at departure. A departure-time model cannot see this risk, which sets a floor on achievable error.

**Port traffic.** `port_traffic_history` is derived from telemetry. `vessel_calls` equals the daily sum of `vessel_arrivals` on all 410 complete days, and `avg_vessel_stay_hours` equals the daily mean vessel time (within 0.05 h). For this reason all `tr_*` columns are excluded.

**Holidays.**
- Holidays coincide with lower utilisation at every port (−4 to −36 pp).
- Spring Festival at CNSHA coincides with +4.3 vessels at anchor and +19 h dwell, with no logged event.
- Departing within 2 days of an origin holiday: 60 % vs 44 % delayed (V = 0.14), or 56 % vs 45 % excluding CNSHA.
- A hub holiday during the voyage has V = 0.13.
- Caveats:
  - The calendar ends 26 Feb, while 267 sea shipments arrive later.
  - `port_closed` is always `False`.
  - Multi-day periods are stored as separate dated rows.

## H. Integrated shipment-level analysis (notebook 08, sections H1–H8)

**Join validation (H1).** The integration yields exactly 9,850 rows, and every join is validated `m:1`. Missingness is only structural:
- no hub on direct routes (62.7 %);
- no arrived history yet (14.7 % of `hist_*`);
- no active event;
- 8 first-day departures with no 24 h change.

**Strongest pre-departure signals (H2)**, as Cliff's δ for Critical vs On-Time:

| Pre-departure condition at origin | On-Time | At-Risk | Critical | δ Crit vs OT | δ AR vs OT |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Active disruption at departure (share) | 5.6 % | 16.9 % | 44.3 % | 0.39 | 0.11 |
| Anchor queue, 24 h mean (median vessels) | 0.42 | 0.67 | 1.92 | **0.54** | 0.31 |
| Container dwell (median h) | 77.6 | 87.7 | 100.7 | 0.44 | 0.19 |
| Max wind gust, prior 24 h (median km/h) | 34.2 | 36.1 | 48.7 | 0.44 | 0.14 |
| Precipitation, prior 24 h (median mm) | 2.2 | 2.9 | 7.7 | 0.44 | 0.14 |
| Vessel time in port (median h) | 23.6 | 25.2 | 27.0 | 0.33 | 0.17 |

Capacity loss of an active origin event has δ 0.72 among the 15 % of shipments that have one. The static attributes add an independent second layer: route type V 0.18, origin 0.16, hub 0.13, carrier 0.12, priority 0.08, cargo 0.07.

**Combinations (H4).**
- No origin event + queue ≤ 2 + direct route → 26 % delayed, 4.5 % Critical.
- Origin event + queue > 2 + transshipment → 92 % delayed, 62 % Critical.
- The EDA recommends this simple rule as the baseline to beat.

**Coverage.**
- 87 % of Critical and 61 % of At-Risk shipments had at least one visible adverse origin condition (active event, anchor queue > 2, gust > 50 km/h, holiday ±2 d), compared with 32 % of On-Time shipments.
- **All 28 extreme sea delays (> 140 h) departed during an active origin disruption.**

**Redundancy (H3).** There are 22 pairs with |ρ| ≥ 0.8 among 48 informative features (figure `08_feature_correlation_clustermap.png`). See §I.

**Leakage contrast (H5).** Future information turned out *weaker* than expected: destination state at arrival has δ ≈ 0, and events starting en route have δ 0.05–0.10. Risk is mostly set by origin conditions at departure.

**Cutoff sensitivity (H6).** Moving the cutoff from 1 h to 24 h before departure has these effects:
- the gust δ drops 0.44 → 0.16 (≈ 0 at 72 h);
- the anchor δ drops 0.54 → 0.32;
- dwell decays least (0.44 → 0.33).

The ML stage later found the same effect in model performance ([04 · §G](04_ML_Model_Evaluation.md#g-robustness-and-statistical-analysis)).

**Stability (H7).** Relationships keep their sign across the three fortnights (anchor ρ 0.31–0.46, gust ρ 0.23–0.30).

**History (H8).** Point-in-time historical outcome rates are weak (origin history δ ≈ 0.02–0.07). Arrivals lag departures by about 10 days, so history reflects past episodes rather than current ones.

## I. Feature assessment (notebook 09, sections I1–I5)

**Column catalogue (I1).** Every integrated column has a category (safe / leakage / needs decision / redundant) and a rationale. It is written to `reports/tables/09_feature_catalog.csv`, which is not present in this checkout (§A). The ML stage keeps its own catalogue in `ML/ML/results/data/feature_catalog.csv`.

**Leakage demonstrations (I2):**

| Demonstration | Result |
|---|---|
| (a) Outcome columns | `actual_arrival_ts`, `actual_delay_hours` and `delay_status` each reproduce the label with 100 % accuracy |
| (b) Naive target encoding | full-data route/origin/destination delay rates look 4–6× stronger than the point-in-time version (route δ 0.43 vs 0.08) |
| (c) Shared episodes under a random split | 99.8 % of random-split test slots have a training shipment from the same origin within ±6 h, so random splits overstate performance |
| (d) Label horizon | at a time cut, 38–49 % of earlier departures have not yet arrived, and these are disproportionately delayed (figure `09_class_mix_by_day_split_points.png`) |

**Columns to exclude:**
- outcomes;
- 17 `LEAK_*` columns;
- `event_end` / remaining duration of ongoing events;
- naive target encodings;
- lineage flags;
- `dep_day_index` / absolute date;
- `shipment_id`.

**Columns that need a decision:**
- `dis_*_max_sev` and `dis_*_capacity_pct` (unknown whether they are known at onset);
- visibility;
- filled-input flags;
- `expected_arrival_ts` for the 196 rows where it was reconstructed.

The ML stage counted 194 such rows among observed shipments ([01 · §C](01_ML_Dataset_Engineering.md#c-data-integration)).

**Redundancy clusters.** Keep one column per cluster:
- `port_utilization_pct` ≡ throughput / capacity;
- `tr_*` ≡ telemetry aggregates;
- `planned_duration_h` ≡ `planned_transit_hours` (sea);
- disruption `active_any` ≈ `active_n` ≈ `active_max_sev` ≈ `started_72h_n` (ρ 0.97–0.999);
- gusts ≈ wind speed (0.97);
- dwell snapshot ≈ dwell 24 h mean (0.95);
- anchor mean ≈ anchor max (0.88);
- active-event capacity ≈ −utilisation (−0.85);
- `route_id` ≡ `od_pair`.

**Recommended feature groups (I4, EDA §5).** These map one-to-one onto the ML `FEATURE_GROUPS` ([01 · §E](01_ML_Dataset_Engineering.md#e-feature-engineering)):
- origin disruption state;
- origin congestion (anchor, dwell, vessel time, utilisation max with the current value);
- origin weather;
- route structure (origin, destination and hub encoded per role);
- hub state;
- shipment attributes;
- calendar;
- point-in-time history.

**Unresolved decisions (I3), documented but not resolved:**
1. the prediction timestamp;
2. `ts_hour` semantics;
3. when disruption attributes become known;
4. whether `departure_ts` is actual or planned;
5. how filled values were produced;
6. how air shipments are handled;
7. ordinal vs nominal treatment of the classes;
8. the label-horizon / retraining policy.

## K. Conclusions

1. **The data is relationally clean.** Keys are unique, foreign keys resolve, timestamps are UTC and hourly series are complete. The biggest data-quality risk is **values filled during cleaning**, especially the 295 unobserved outcomes, which must be excluded.
2. **Signal is strong and available before departure.** It comes mainly from time-varying origin conditions (active disruption, anchor queue, gusts, precipitation, dwell) and secondarily from route structure, carrier, priority and cargo.
3. **At-Risk is the hardest class.** Nearly every feature separates At-Risk from On-Time far less than Critical from On-Time.
4. **Leakage risks are concrete:** outcome columns, future state, remaining event duration, naive target encoding, random splits (shared episodes) and the label horizon. These lead to the cutoff, exclusion and purge rules in [01 · §F–G](01_ML_Dataset_Engineering.md#f-leakage-prevention-and-prediction-cutoff).
5. **The EDA's readiness verdict was "ready for predictive modelling, with conditions":**
   - decide the prediction timestamp;
   - exclude the 295 unobserved outcomes;
   - use time-based validation with a purge gap of at least the label horizon, never random splits.

   The ML stage adopted the last two and kept `departure_ts − 1 h` as a convention, leaving the timestamp decision open.
6. **The ML stage's design follows the EDA recommendations directly:**
   - sea-only metrics reported;
   - air kept in the model;
   - ordinal, cost-aware metrics (Critical → On-Time rate, ordinal MAE);
   - class weights with no oversampling;
   - one column per redundancy cluster.
